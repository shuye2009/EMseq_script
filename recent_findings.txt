1. Molecular Mechanisms of the FLASH Effect
The oxygen depletion hypothesis and other potential mechanisms (like radical recombination and immune modulation) driving the tissue-sparing effect of ultra-high dose rate (FLASH) radiotherapy have been extensively reviewed and debated recently:

Zhang et al. (2024). "Current views on mechanisms of the FLASH effect in cancer radiotherapy". National Science Review, 11(10), nwae350.
Focus: Comprehensive review evaluating the radiolytic oxygen depletion hypothesis versus other emerging theories like mitochondrial dynamics and DNA integrity preservation.
Abolfath et al. (2024). "The oxygen puzzle in FLASH radiotherapy: A comprehensive review". Physics and Imaging in Radiation Oncology, 31, 100537.
Focus: Deep dive into how transient hypoxia and reactive oxygen species (ROS) dynamics act as the primary biological drivers of normal tissue sparing.
2. cGAS-STING Pathway and Fractionated Dose Optimization
The fine-tuning of radiation dosing to optimally trigger the cGAS-STING pathway—maximizing systemic immune responses (abscopal effect) without ablating the immune cells themselves—has been a major focus:

Galluzzi et al. (2023 / 2024). "Harnessing the cGAS-STING pathway to potentiate radiation therapy: Translating preclinical promise into clinical reality". Frontiers in Pharmacology, 15, 1383000.
Focus: Reviews the molecular mechanisms linking radiation-induced DNA double-strand breaks to cytosolic micronuclei formation, cGAMP production, and optimal fractionation schedules to maximize abscopal responses.
Baskar et al. (2023). "The Abscopal Effect of Radiation Therapy". Future Oncology.
Focus: Details how optimal radiation doses are strictly necessary to promote the cGAS-STING pathway without triggering immunosuppressive cascades.
3. Spatial Transcriptomics and the Radioresistant Tumor Microenvironment
The use of spatial transcriptomics and advanced sequencing to map how tumors reorganize their microenvironments (TME) to survive radiation is a rapidly expanding area:

Liu et al. (2024). "A critical review on exploring tumor Microenvironment's impacts on radioresistance". Radiotherapy and Oncology.
Focus: Reviews how the TME—specifically through interactions with cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) and tumor-associated macrophages—fosters radioresistant niches.
Wu et al. (2023). "Mechanisms and perspective treatment of radioresistance in cancer". Frontiers in Immunology, 14, 1133899.
Focus: Synthesizes how irradiated tumor cells undergo epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and secrete signals that reprogram surrounding stromal cells into a protective state to survive radiotherapy.
